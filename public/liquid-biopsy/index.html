Rosa Bio · P2G · liquid biopsy

P2G provides robust liquid biopsy prediction across all analyte types.

Last updated 2026-11-11 08:52 PST

P2G builds a predictive model directly from the raw sequencing reads of each sample: alignment performed after model predictions, no gene list. The analysis is performed with the same modeling, no difference between RNA or DNA, between or base-conversion or native nucleic acid sequences. No study-specific optimization. All predictions are reported on data never seen during training.

Main point 1

P2G finds all 16 fusions in the reference mix and predicts the tumour DNA percent of 38 held-out libraries (R² 0.93)

Question: does P2G find tumour signals in reference samples where the answer is known before the test?

These are reference samples, not patients: lab-made cell-line RNA or DNA with known fusions and mutations, mixed into normal RNA or DNA at set amounts. They check that P2G finds a known answer before the patient studies below.

Reference samples · fusion RNA · Seraseq Fusion RNA Mix v3 (SRP293279)

All 16 fusions in the reference mix are found with no model training, 9 already at a 1% spike-in, and none in 277 control samples

18 libraries: the Seraseq Fusion RNA Mix v3 reference diluted into normal RNA, 3 replicate libraries at each of 6 spike-in levels, 101 to 227 million read pairs per library (median 159 million). 277 control samples with no spiked fusion: 90 plasma RNA (7.3 billion reads) and 187 tumour DNA libraries (42.2 billion reads). No stage: lab-made reference material, not patients.

No training. No model was trained, and the method was not told which fusions are in the mix: it searched every read for 41 known fusion junctions and reports the ones it finds. It was also not told which libraries were spiked, or at what level.

Two fusions marked on the chart. KIF5B-RET and SLC45A3-BRAF are the only fusions found in all 3 replicate libraries at the 5% spike-in, and both are already found at 1%.

Pregnancy · maternal plasma RNA · SRP271730

Adverse pregnancy outcomes are told apart from uncomplicated pregnancies at AUC 1.000; the paper reports 0.91

Cases: preeclampsia, gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes or chronic hypertension. Controls: pregnancies with no complication. 26 test samples (17 patients, 9 in the comparison group) from women kept out of training. P2G used 2 million of a median 32.7 million reads per sample (6.1%). Not a cancer, so there is no stage.

The paper (Del Vecchio 2021) reports AUC 0.91 for adverse outcomes from first-trimester plasma RNA, by leave-one-out cross-validation on 23 women (15 with an adverse outcome, 8 normal).

Definitions

Terms used on this page

AUC
How well a score separates two groups: 1 is perfect, 0.5 is no better than a coin flip.
95% CI next to 1.000
The range the AUC would likely fall in with more samples of the same kind (Newcombe 2006).
Test samples
Samples kept out of training and scored only once, at the end. Written as N test samples (cases, controls).
Sensitivity at 95% specificity
The percent of cases found when the cutoff is set so that 95% of controls are called negative.
Cross-study
A model fitted in one study and applied unchanged to all samples of another study.
Selected sequence and model weight
A short read sequence the model uses; its weight is how much the model relies on it, as a percent of the whole model.
Tumour DNA percent
How much of a library's DNA comes from the SEQC2 reference tumour DNA (0%, 0.8%, 4% or 20%).
R²
How much of the spread in the true values the predictions explain: 1 is all of it.
Spike-in
A known amount of reference material mixed into a sample, so the right answer is known before the test.
Sensitivity CI
The range the percent of cases found would likely fall in with more cases (Wilson).
Inside the model

Known in the literature
